# Deep Work Worked Example: A Week That Fits the Job

**Fictional teaching example.** All commitments, interruptions and work outputs are invented. The records illustrate planning and review; they do not report an observed productivity gain.

## The output and the fixed calendar

A founder needs a decision memo comparing manual setup with guided setup for LessonLoop's next pilot. The memo should identify the customer problem, compare both options against current constraints, recommend one and record an unresolved risk. Writing this comparison requires sustained reasoning; inbox processing does not answer it.

Working hours are Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00. Lunch is 12:00–13:00. Customer support is 13:00–15:00 each day. Wednesday also has on-call duty from 09:00–10:00 with no backup. Meetings are Monday 10:30–11:30, Tuesday 09:00–10:00, Thursday 10:00–11:00 and Friday 15:00–16:00. These are supplied fictional constraints, not movable empty space.

Available inputs are three customer-note excerpts N1–N3, the current product capability list and a rough setup-time estimate. No customer has tested the proposed guided flow. The first action is to extract the decision criteria before writing a polished introduction.

Total planned focus: 285 minutes. The Wednesday block overlaps on-call duty and is not protected. Plan A wrongly assumes the inbox being closed means the responsibility disappears. The schedule should have caught this before the week began; the example retains it to show how to diagnose the failure.

The initial preparation rule is to open N1–N3 and the capability list before each session. Routine replies wait for the support window. An assigned incident remains interrupting work. Nothing in this plan disables alerts or changes a live calendar.

The memo is unfinished despite five attempted blocks. More time is not the only issue: the work sequence allowed polishing before the decision existed, and the interrupted session had no useful resume note.

## The decision criteria in the draft

The supplied fictional notes support these inputs:

- N1: one tutor could maintain the list after seeing an example.
- N2: one tutor got stuck deciding which fields to enter first.
- N3: one tutor did not want ongoing manual updates at all.
- Product fact: replies are not synchronized automatically.
- Delivery estimate: the founder can support a small assisted pilot, but has no evidence that a guided interface removes the onboarding problem.

N3 limits the recommendation. A better setup flow does not solve rejection of manual upkeep. The memo should keep that objection visible rather than use a focus session to argue around it.

## Diagnosis and chosen changes

The Wednesday problem is responsibility overlap. Move the demanding work away from the on-call window; do not invent backup. Monday's problem is preparation. Pin the correct notes before the session. Thursday's loss is a missing resume note. Friday's problem is output sequencing: draft the recommendation before cosmetic improvements.

Tuesday's message checking suggests an unnecessary switch. The proposed rule is to capture the thought and return to the task until the next scheduled response time, while retaining genuine incident alerts. The founder does not block the browser because the source notes require it.

## Resume and shutdown notes

Example interruption note: “Compared manual setup with N1/N2. Still need to address N3's rejection of upkeep. Next action: write why assisted setup helps initial entry but does not establish ongoing fit.”

End-of-session note: “Memo saved in the project decision folder. Recommendation drafted; claim about lower support time remains unsupported. Next session: remove or label that estimate, then define the pilot question. Routine replies remain in today's support window.”

These notes retain the next thinking step without extending the session into lunch or evening. If the next step needs more time than the plan allows, scope or timing must change explicitly.

## Completed illustrative memo

**Decision:** Use a small assisted manual setup for the next proposed pilot rather than build a guided setup interface immediately.

**Reason:** N1 suggests an example may be sufficient for some users; N2 identifies initial field choice as an obstacle. Assisted setup can expose where people struggle without first assuming that a new interface is the answer.

**Alternative:** A guided interface might reduce repeated explanation, but no supplied test establishes that benefit. Building it now would commit development effort before the specific sticking points are understood.

**Limit:** N3's rejection of manual upkeep remains unresolved. Assisted setup should not be offered as automation or treated as a solution for every tutor.

**Next proposed check:** Observe a small authorized pilot's setup steps and recurring questions, while separately asking whether ongoing maintenance is acceptable. Record time and errors; do not claim a support reduction before observing it.

This is the example's finished artifact, written from its fictional inputs. It is not evidence that plan B ran or made the founder more productive.

## Review the plan and the output separately

Plan B is calendar-feasible under the supplied commitments. Its actual focused minutes are unknown. The illustrative memo meets the stated decision criteria, but its business hypothesis remains untested. Those are three separate conclusions.

If the next real week still produces only polished background, begin the next session at the missing decision and reduce scope. If incidents consume even the revised blocks, renegotiate coverage or timeline where possible; do not quietly prescribe evening catch-up.
