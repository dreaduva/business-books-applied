A visitor should be able to tell who a page is for, what it helps them do, and what happens after clicking its main button. This toolkit turns a messaging discussion into a **landing-page draft you can review line by line**.

## Summary

Donald Miller's StoryBrand framework puts the customer at the center of the story and the business in a supporting role. Its seven elements connect a customer's goal and problem with a guide, a plan, a call to action, the consequences of leaving the problem unresolved, and a successful outcome. The practical lesson is clarity: describe the customer's situation and a comprehensible way forward instead of making the visitor decipher a company biography.

## From framework to page

The worksheet below is our original writing tool, not a reproduction of the official BrandScript. It asks for five concrete outputs: audience and problem, supportable credibility, a path to use the product, publishable copy, and a review plan.

Start with one visitor and one primary action. A page asking someone to book a demonstration needs to explain the meeting; a self-service signup page needs to explain access, payment, and setup. “Get started” can conceal these differences. Use a button label that tells the visitor what actually happens.

| Page element | Question to answer | Example for a confirmation tracker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | What can I do here? | See which lesson changes still need a reply |
| Supporting line | Is this relevant to my situation? | A pending-confirmation view for independent tutors |
| Demonstration | Why believe the description? | A labeled screenshot of the real product |
| Steps | What effort is required? | Add a change, record a reply, review pending items |
| Primary action | What happens next? | Request a walkthrough |

## Make the claims smaller and stronger

If the product displays unresolved confirmations, say that. “Never lose a customer again” is broader than the product can support. Testimonials must come from actual customers with permission to use them; a new product can show a functioning demonstration instead. Do not turn a fictional example from this repository into social proof.

Describe the cost of an unresolved problem proportionately. A missed reply is an operational inconvenience; it does not justify telling visitors their business will collapse. Clarity works better when the reader can judge the consequence for themselves.

## A useful review before publishing

Read only the headline, supporting line, and button. Can a new reader explain the offer without guessing? Then check the setup steps against the real product. Finally, highlight every factual claim and attach its evidence or remove it. Test comprehension separately from conversion: someone understanding the page does not establish that they will buy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a StoryBrand template?** It is an independent worksheet for applying customer-centered messaging. The official framework and workbook are linked below.

**Does every page need all seven elements as headings?** No. Use them to examine the message; choose a page structure that suits the decision and reading context.

**Which edition is pictured?** The original 2017 edition. This toolkit does not claim to summarize additions in Building a StoryBrand 2.0.

**What comes first?** Resolve the buyer and comparison in [Obviously Awesome](../obviously-awesome/README.md). Messaging cannot supply missing product differentiation.
