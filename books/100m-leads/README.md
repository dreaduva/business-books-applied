A contact list is not a pipeline. This toolkit helps you define whom you want to reach, what useful first interaction you will offer, and how to tell a qualified response from a view or download.

## Summary

Alex Hormozi's lead-generation material connects four broad routes: warm outreach, publishing content, cold outreach, and paid advertising. The accompanying course also develops lead magnets, referrals, and working with others to generate leads. The useful starting point is to make an offer visible to suitable people and give interested people a clear next step. More attention is only helpful when it can lead to a relevant commercial conversation.

## Choose a route for this campaign

Use the Core Four as a map, not an instruction to do everything at once. Your existing relationships, audience access, budget, and ability to follow up determine which route is feasible. This worksheet plans one campaign after that choice. For a wider channel comparison, use [Traction](../traction-gabriel-weinberg/README.md).

| Route | Starting advantage | Question before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm outreach | An existing relationship | Is this offer relevant in that relationship? |
| Content | A useful answer that can be shared | Where will suitable readers encounter it? |
| Cold outreach | A specific, researched fit | Is the contact method appropriate and permitted? |
| Paid advertising | Purchased distribution | Can you measure outcomes and afford the test? |

## Make the entry resource useful on its own

A lead magnet should solve a small, relevant problem. A tutor-facing confirmation checklist might help someone identify unresolved schedule changes even if they never buy software. Keep its promise narrow and deliver the promised resource before asking for more effort.

Explain what happens if someone provides an email address. Receiving a resource and agreeing to continuing marketing are separate choices. This toolkit is a campaign draft; it does not authorize scraping contacts, sending messages, or enrolling anyone in a mailing list.

## Define a qualified response

Write the qualification rule before interpreting results. For our fictional tutor product, a relevant response comes from someone currently handling changing lesson times manually who wants to discuss that workflow. A student downloading a checklist may appreciate it without being a prospective customer.

Track exposures, resource use, explicit interest, qualified conversations, and purchases as separate stages. Preserve the denominator of each rate. A download-to-conversation rate cannot be compared with an impression-to-purchase rate as though they measure the same thing.

## Follow up without creating a nuisance

Give one owner responsibility for replies and the capacity to handle them. Set a reasonable proposed follow-up boundary and honor opt-outs immediately. If the campaign attracts the wrong people, revise the audience or resource before increasing volume. If it attracts suitable people but nobody can reply, fix the handoff first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a lead the same as a customer?** No. State exactly which stage your count represents; contactability, engagement, qualification, and purchase are different facts.

**Must a lead magnet collect an email?** No. An ungated resource can be useful and invite an optional next step. Choose the interaction deliberately.

**Is more outreach always better?** No. Relevance, permission, deliverability, and follow-up capacity constrain a responsible campaign.

Start with a deliverable offer using [$100M Offers](../100m-offers/README.md). A campaign cannot compensate for an offer the business cannot fulfill.
