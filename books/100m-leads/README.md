A contact list is not a pipeline. This toolkit helps you define whom you want to reach, what useful first interaction you will offer, and how to tell a qualified response from a view or download.

## Summary

Alex Hormozi's lead-generation material connects four broad routes: warm outreach, publishing content, cold outreach, and paid advertising. The accompanying course also develops lead magnets, referrals, and working with others to generate leads. The useful starting point is to make an offer visible to suitable people and give interested people a clear next step. More attention is only helpful when it can lead to a relevant commercial conversation.

## Choose a route for this campaign

Use the Core Four as a map, not an instruction to do everything at once. Your existing relationships, audience access, budget, and ability to follow up determine which route is feasible. This worksheet plans one campaign after that choice. For a wider channel comparison, use [Traction](../traction-gabriel-weinberg/README.md).

| Route | Starting advantage | Question before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm outreach | An existing relationship | Is this offer relevant in that relationship? |
| Content | A useful answer that can be shared | Where will suitable readers encounter it? |
| Cold outreach | A specific, researched fit | Is the contact method appropriate and permitted? |
| Paid advertising | Purchased distribution | Can you measure outcomes and afford the test? |

## Make the entry resource useful on its own

A lead magnet should solve a small, relevant problem. A tutor-facing confirmation checklist might help someone identify unresolved schedule changes even if they never buy software. Keep its promise narrow and deliver the promised resource before asking for more effort.

Explain what happens if someone provides an email address. Receiving a resource and agreeing to continuing marketing are separate choices. This toolkit is a campaign draft; it does not authorize scraping contacts, sending messages, or enrolling anyone in a mailing list.

## Define a qualified response

Write the qualification rule before interpreting results. For our fictional tutor product, a relevant response comes from someone currently handling changing lesson times manually who wants to discuss that workflow. A student downloading a checklist may appreciate it without being a prospective customer.

Track exposures, resource use, explicit interest, qualified conversations, and purchases as separate stages. Preserve the denominator of each rate. A download-to-conversation rate cannot be compared with an impression-to-purchase rate as though they measure the same thing.

## Follow up without creating a nuisance

Give one owner responsibility for replies and the capacity to handle them. Set a reasonable proposed follow-up boundary and honor opt-outs immediately. If the campaign attracts the wrong people, revise the audience or resource before increasing volume. If it attracts suitable people but nobody can reply, fix the handoff first.

## Turn a route into an actual campaign

The [official course index](https://www.acquisition.com/training/leads) separates resources, outreach, content, ads and ways other people can help generate leads. That is useful orientation, but an index is not a complete campaign. The operating method below is our original application: define one audience encounter, deliver something useful, identify relevant interest and make the next conversation possible.

### Choose an encounter, not just a channel label

Warm outreach starts with a relationship; that relationship does not prove the offer fits. Content starts with something worth reading; it still needs a credible distribution path. Cold outreach needs a reason this person and this offer belong together. Ads can buy exposure but cannot repair an undefined offer or absent follow-up capacity.

Write where a suitable person will encounter the resource and what they will be doing at the time. A tutor association's lesson-planning discussion is a more specific context than “social media.” If access depends on a moderator or partner, record that dependency before preparing a large campaign.

Choose one route you can execute and evaluate. If the question is which route deserves attention, use [Traction](../traction-gabriel-weinberg/README.md). Here the question is how to make the selected encounter useful and how to handle what follows. A new headline within the same route is an iteration, not evidence that every acquisition channel has been tested.

### Make the first benefit complete

A useful entry resource solves a small problem without requiring a purchase to finish it. For tutors, a one-page list of unresolved lesson changes can have value independently of software. Provide enough instructions and an example row for the person to use it. “Download our ultimate guide” is not a resource specification.

Check the relationship to the commercial offer. A general productivity checklist may attract students, office workers and other people with no need for tutoring software. A narrower lesson-change worksheet gives fewer people a reason to open it but makes the next discussion more coherent. Volume and relevance need separate measurement.

The next step should extend the same problem. After helping someone identify unresolved replies, offer to demonstrate a tool for maintaining that list. Do not switch abruptly to an unrelated service. Also allow the reader to stop with the resource; useful content is not a promise to purchase.

### Draft the promise and the destination together

Write the distribution message, resource introduction and next-step invitation as one small journey. Specify what is free, whether an email is requested, and what a request triggers. The destination must deliver the promised resource rather than another vague pitch.

Use true relevance in the message. “You mentioned managing changed lesson times in this conversation” is acceptable only when that conversation actually happened. If you do not have that context, omit it. A plausible invented detail can make a draft feel personal while making it unusable.

Keep any broader marketing subscription separate from resource delivery or a requested reply. For this worksheet, document the person's requested interaction and proposed follow-up boundary. Actual distribution requires the user's authorization and applicable channel requirements; a plan is not permission to scrape or send.

## Build a funnel whose stages mean something

Define the events in words before creating a spreadsheet. A resource open is an interaction with content. An explicit request to discuss the product is interest in another step. Qualification evaluates fit using a rule established before results arrive. A held conversation requires attendance, and a sale requires an actual purchase.

A person can be relevant without requesting a conversation, and can request a conversation without being relevant. Track those facts separately if your campaign needs to understand both. Do not infer fit from a download or count the same person repeatedly because they opened the file several times.

Choose one denominator for each rate. If 100 people open a resource and five ask for a call, request/open is 5%. If three of those five fit the audience, qualified/request is 60%; qualified/open is 3%. All can be accurate, but each answers a different question. A report should retain the counts so a reader can reconstruct the meaning.

Follow-up windows matter. A request made at the end of the reporting period may not have had time to receive a reply or book a call. Mark it pending rather than silently treating it as a lost lead. A funnel leak cannot be diagnosed solely from a snapshot with mixed maturity.

## Own the handoff before increasing volume

Assign a named owner or role, a response expectation the team can meet, and capacity for the proposed number of conversations. The owner needs the person's requested next step and enough context to respond appropriately. A download list without that context is a poor handoff.

Draft the acknowledgement, useful first reply and any bounded follow-up. Each should answer the next practical question, not repeat a sales pitch. If someone declines or asks to stop, record that and close the sequence. If the owner is unavailable, reduce the invitation volume or state the actual response timing rather than advertising instant help.

Track delays separately from audience quality. Relevant people who never received a response do not demonstrate a weak offer. Before buying more exposure, inspect whether the existing people got the promised interaction.

## Diagnose before choosing more volume

| Pattern | Possible explanation | Evidence to inspect | Revision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Many opens, few relevant requests | Resource attracts the wrong job | Requester roles and problems | Narrow the promise or context |
| Suitable readers, little next-step interest | Resource is sufficient or offer is weak | Actual objections and offered next step | Clarify the connection; investigate need |
| Requests wait unanswered | Ownership/capacity failure | Response timestamps | Fix handoff before distribution |
| Calls booked but not held | Timing or commitment unclear | Booking confirmation and stated expectations | Repair the scheduling flow |
| Calls held but no purchases | Offer, price, trust or fit unresolved | Conversation evidence | Return to offer design; do not assume one cause |
| Counts look strong but IDs overlap | Duplicate measurement | Unique people and event definitions | Rebuild the funnel before acting |

Choose the smallest revision that tests the strongest explanation. If the audience is wrong, changing the resource title and every follow-up message at once makes learning harder. Preserve a version record with what changed and why. A revised plan is a hypothesis until it runs.

Set a cap and review point for the next attempt. A sensible outcome may be a few better-qualified conversations, an explicit mismatch, or discovery that the offer is not ready. None requires pretending downloads were customers. Keep the commercial result and the resource's usefulness visible as separate outcomes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a lead the same as a customer?** No. State exactly which stage your count represents; contactability, engagement, qualification, and purchase are different facts.

**Must a lead magnet collect an email?** No. An ungated resource can be useful and invite an optional next step. Choose the interaction deliberately.

**Is more outreach always better?** No. Relevance, permission, deliverability, and follow-up capacity constrain a responsible campaign.

Start with a deliverable offer using [$100M Offers](../100m-offers/README.md). A campaign cannot compensate for an offer the business cannot fulfill.
