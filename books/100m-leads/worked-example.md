# $100M Leads Worked Example

**Fictional teaching example.** All organizations, participants, numbers, and outcomes are invented for this exercise, not reported results or a case from the book.

## Audience and qualification

Fictional LessonLoop targets independent tutors handling changing lessons through manual messages. The next offer is a walkthrough of a confirmation tracker. Qualified response: a tutor describes that routine and explicitly asks to discuss the tool. Students and tutors already satisfied with automated booking are outside the initial campaign.

## Route and useful resource

Choose content distributed through one moderator-approved tutor community post. Resource: a free, ungated checklist for finding unresolved lesson changes. It explains how to list changed times, mark replies, and revisit unresolved items. It remains usable without LessonLoop; an optional walkthrough link is relevant to readers who want a persistent view.

## Draft and next step

Draft: “When lesson times change, it can be hard to see which replies are still missing. Here is a short checklist for reviewing pending confirmations using your existing notes. If you want to see a tool for keeping that view, you can request a LessonLoop walkthrough.” Destination: the checklist, with an optional request form. Draft only; moderator permission is a prerequisite and no post has been sent.

## Follow-up and capacity

Owner: fictional founder Maya, with capacity for four walkthroughs in the test week. Reply only to explicit requests through the form. Proposed boundary: one scheduling reminder if a requester has not responded, then close the request. Any opt-out stops contact. The resource itself requires no email and adds nobody to a marketing list.

## Measurement and decision

Illustrative results: 80 resource visits, 6 walkthrough requests, 4 qualified requests, 3 attended walkthroughs, 1 pilot purchase. Qualified-request rate: 4/80 = 5%; purchase per attended walkthrough: 1/3, a very small sample. These invented counts demonstrate calculation only. Proposed test cap: four hours, no paid distribution. Review fit and support workload before repeating; do not extrapolate revenue from one purchase.
