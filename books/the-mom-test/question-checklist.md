# Review Your Customer Interview Questions

[Book guide](README.md) · [Interview template](../../templates/customer-interview-plan.md)

An original practice tool from Business Books, Applied, informed by the approach discussed in *The Mom Test*. This is not an official worksheet or a validated scoring instrument.

## Review one question at a time

| Check | Ask yourself | Revision if needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant | Does this answer inform the decision we need to make? | Remove interesting but irrelevant questions. |
| Concrete | Can the participant describe something that happened? | Ask for a recent example before asking for an explanation. |
| Neutral | Have I assumed the task is difficult, expensive, or broken? | Remove the assumption and ask how the situation unfolded. |
| Open | Could the answer weaken my preferred idea? | Rewrite a question that only invites agreement. |
| Focused | Am I asking several things at once? | Ask one question and use follow-ups. |
| Useful | Will I learn about behavior, a workaround, or a consequence? | Replace approval-seeking with a question about the current situation. |

## Try the check

**Original:** “Wouldn't automatic confirmations save you hours every week?”

This assumes the problem is frequent, costs hours, and would be fixed by automation. It also asks the participant to predict an outcome.

**Revision:** “What happened after the lesson time changed?”

**Follow-ups:** “How did everyone find out?” and “How does that compare with a normal week?”

The revised question can reveal that the existing process works well. Keep that answer in your notes.
