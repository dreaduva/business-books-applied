# Atomic Habits Worked Example: Useful Notes, Not Just a Streak

**Fictional teaching example.** The founder, calendar, observations and results are invented. This is a work-routine exercise, not a scientific study or a real customer's habit record.

## The intention and the first design

A founder wants customer discussions to inform product decisions. The current intention is “review customer notes every workday.” Five working days per week are scheduled; weekends are outside the planned routine. The proposed cue is closing the final work call. The minimum is opening the notes and writing one sentence. A check mark records completion.

The founder likes finishing with a clear record rather than carrying every conversation in memory. The environment change is a bookmarked notes folder. The initial two-week window is for observing fit and usefulness, not a claim that a habit forms in fourteen days.

This first minimum is deliberately flawed: a sentence can be completed without preserving anything specific. The review will examine that flaw instead of rewarding a perfect-looking tracker.

Version 1 has seven completions across ten planned opportunities. Only three outputs meet the review's usefulness criterion: concrete observed behavior or a stated customer response, with a traceable source. That is 3/7 completed notes and 3/10 planned opportunities. The review criterion is being introduced now; do not pretend it was the original completion rule.

## What the raw notes support

N2, N5 and N7 are fictional source identifiers. N2 supports a question about tracking changes across threads. N5 shows a paper workaround that should remain in the alternatives list. N7 is contrary evidence against assuming every tutor wants another manual tool. These examples show why useful capture differs from writing something positive every day.

“Customers want simplicity” lacks a specific event and source. “Automation would help” is the founder's interpretation, not an observed need. The correct repair is to inspect the actual source if available, or label the interpretation. Inventing a quotation after the fact would corrupt the record while improving the streak.

## Diagnosis before redesign

Three problems appear, and they are different:

1. The daily opportunity rule and end-of-call cue do not align on Friday 1.
2. Wednesday interruptions leave no reliable transition.
3. The one-sentence minimum rewards vague entries as much as usable evidence.

The founder rejects a more elaborate tracker because it adds work without fixing any of these. A stricter promise to “never miss” also leaves the missing cue and shallow minimum unchanged.

## Version 2: the revised experiment

**Purpose:** Preserve one traceable piece of customer evidence after each customer conversation.

**Opportunity:** Each completed customer conversation, not every workday. Internal calls and days without customer calls are outside this new definition. Keep version 1's ten-opportunity denominator unchanged in its historical report.

**Cue:** The customer call ends. Before the call starts, open its note and the two-field capture card.

**Smallest useful action:** Write one concrete observation or clearly labeled customer statement, then link its source. If there is no relevant observation, record that honestly rather than inventing a useful-looking fact.

**Feedback:** Mark “captured” only after both fields are present. Separately note whether the record helped a later question or decision. The immediate reward is a closed evidence task, not a compulsory extra work session.

**Fallback:** If another obligation begins immediately, leave a short reminder in the source note and use the next available planned review gap that day. Mark it delayed. If no gap exists, record a miss and return at the next opportunity; do not reconstruct details beyond what the saved source supports.

**Boundary:** The minimum does not require a full interview debrief. An optional longer review is separate. If the job routinely needs more than the available gap, schedule it explicitly rather than disguising it as a tiny habit.

**Next review:** After five customer conversations. Inspect completion, source quality, delayed entries and whether any record improved a follow-up question. This is a proposed observation window, not a formation deadline.

## Filled capture card

**Observation:** Tutor C said they already maintain a paper list and did not want another manual list.

**Source:** Fictional note N7, response about current workaround and switching effort.

**Interpretation, kept separate:** Our proposed tracker may add work for tutors satisfied with paper.

**Possible next question:** What, if anything, becomes difficult when several lessons change in the same week?

This card preserves a contrary response. It does not convert a single customer's statement into a market-wide conclusion.

## Rehearsing an interrupted opportunity

In a fictional desk rehearsal, the next conversation ends two minutes before an internal meeting. The source note is already open. The founder records a short reminder, then completes the observation and source link in a later existing review gap. The entry is marked delayed, not immediate. This demonstrates the proposed fallback logic; it is not a newly observed week of improved behavior.

Version 2 changes both opportunity definition and minimum quality. A future increase cannot be attributed solely to one of them, and its completion rate is not directly comparable to the original daily rate. Keep both raw logs and explain the changed rule.

## Keep, change or stop

Keep the smaller, source-linked action if it fits actual conversations and produces usable evidence. Change the fallback if delayed capture repeatedly lacks enough context. Stop requiring a daily review when there is nothing relevant to review. If capture is consistent but no one uses the notes, examine the downstream decision process instead of demanding a longer streak.

No claim about automaticity, revenue or personal discipline follows from these fictional two weeks.
