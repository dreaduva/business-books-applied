**This is Traction by Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, the customer-acquisition book.** It is different from Gino Wickman's book about running a business.

## Summary

Weinberg and Mares describe nineteen acquisition channels and a selection approach called Bullseye. The central idea is to consider a broad set of possibilities, investigate promising routes, and concentrate effort where evidence supports it. Product development alone does not create a reliable path to customers. The appropriate route depends on the business and its stage; a channel's popularity does not establish its usefulness for your offer.

## A Bullseye worksheet you can run

Our original worksheet turns that broad-to-focused approach into a test record. First define a meaningful acquisition event. For a paid product, a signup may only be an intermediate event. For a free tool, an activated user may be appropriate. Keep that definition stable while comparing tests.

Next, write a reason each candidate could reach the intended audience. Include routes outside your usual habits. Then choose a small shortlist you can actually evaluate within your cash and time limits. The worksheet does not prescribe a universal number of tests or a universal success threshold.

| Candidate | Why it might fit | Small test | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specialist newsletter | Readers share the relevant job | One approved placement | One issue may not represent the channel |
| Search content | People already seek help with the task | One useful answer page | Organic discovery takes time |
| Partner demonstration | A trusted provider reaches likely users | One agreed session | Partner access may be hard to repeat |

## Compare outcomes without fooling yourself

Record cash, labor, exposure, qualified responses, activation, and paid customers separately. “Cost per signup” is not customer acquisition cost. A cash-free channel can still require substantial founder time. If you estimate a value for that time, show it as an assumption and preserve the raw hours.

Small tests are especially vulnerable to attribution errors. A buyer who saw a newsletter and later searched your brand should not quietly count as two customers. State the attribution rule and what you cannot track. Do not infer that the lowest observed cost will remain constant when spending increases.

## Deciding what to try next

A useful test can reveal that a channel reaches the wrong people, that the offer is unclear, or that the measurement is broken. Those require different responses. If no one saw the offer, you learned little about demand. If qualified visitors arrived but did not understand it, revisit the message before blaming the channel.

Choose a next test with a specific reason, budget ceiling, and review point. Focusing means making a deliberate allocation, not declaring a permanent winner.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does this worksheet list all nineteen channels?** No. The book develops the full channel landscape. This application helps compare and test candidates relevant to your situation.

**Can I compare SEO with a one-day advertisement?** Not on immediate results alone. Their time horizons differ; record that limitation and use appropriate observation windows.

**Do clicks prove traction?** They show an early interaction. Define the behavior that matters for your business and follow the funnel to that point.
