# How we write and review

Write something a founder would keep open while doing the work. A fluent summary is not enough: the guide should help the reader make a better decision or produce something useful.

## Voice

Start with a recognizable situation. Explain the idea in plain language. Use specific examples, varied sentence lengths, and the vocabulary the task actually needs. Prefer a useful distinction to another list of generic takeaways.

For example, replace “This transformative framework empowers entrepreneurs to unlock customer insights” with “If every interview ends with a compliment, you may be pitching too early.” The second sentence identifies a problem the reader can notice.

The page template is a navigation aid, not a sentence template. A biography may need a careful discussion of context; a book about interviews may need annotated questions. Let the subject determine the shape and length.

## Originality and evidence

- Read and cite legitimate primary sources: the book, an authorized excerpt, author materials, or publisher materials. Record which were actually consulted.
- Distinguish the author's idea from this project's interpretation or exercise. Do not imply an original template is an official companion workbook.
- Explain where an idea helps and where it can fail. Criticism needs reasons, not a compulsory “cons” section.
- Use direct quotations sparingly, with exact attribution. Do not reproduce chapters, paid worksheets, diagrams, or a substitute for the full book.
- Label fictional examples. Never invent customers, interviews, test results, first-person experience, reading history, credentials, endorsements, or reviewer names.
- Cite consequential claims near the claim. An edition or publication date needs a source if included.

## AI assistance and human judgment

AI may help research, draft, reorganize, and check material. Natural prose comes from specific analysis and editing, not from trying to evade an AI detector. We do not promise detector scores or falsely describe generated content as entirely human-written.

Substantial AI assistance is disclosed in the guide's editorial note. A completed guide can be published after its sources, examples, links, and presentation have been checked, with the scope of that work stated. A human-reviewed label is added only after a named person has actually done that review. Publication and human review are separate facts; automated checks are never described as human editorial review.

The Mom Test guide was prepared with AI assistance from the public author materials and interview listed on its page. It is not represented as a full-book reading, human expert review, or independent field test.

## Review checklist

1. Does the opening tell a particular reader why this guide matters?
2. Are book claims supported, and are original interpretations distinguishable?
3. Does the example demonstrate a decision rather than merely restate the idea?
4. Can the reader use the template or skill without buying anything?
5. Are weak evidence, uncertainty, and important limits visible?
6. Is the main search phrase used naturally, without repetitive variants?
7. Are authorship, AI assistance, review status, cover rights, and affiliate disclosure accurate?
8. Does the page work as normal GitHub Markdown on mobile?
