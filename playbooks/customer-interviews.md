# Customer Interviews: Prepare, Run, Review, Decide

[Home](../README.md) · [All playbooks](README.md) · [The Mom Test guide](../books/the-mom-test/README.md)

**Start with:** a decision, a possible customer segment, and whatever evidence you already have.

**Finish with:** a usable interview script, traceable notes, and a justified next investigation. Use this page as your working sequence; use the book guide for the explanation behind it.

## 1. Prepare the plan

Copy the [interview plan](../books/the-mom-test/interview-plan.md). Name the decision before writing questions. For example: “Should we investigate parent-mediated lesson changes before building a calendar?”

List the assumptions that could make the idea unnecessary. Choose participants with relevant recent experience, and record who your recruitment route misses. If you recruit only people reporting difficulties, you can explore those difficulties but cannot estimate how common they are.

Write actual spoken questions in every conversation field. Use the [question checklist](../books/the-mom-test/question-checklist.md) to remove assumed pain, future promises, and product pitches. The [completed plan](../books/the-mom-test/worked-example.md) shows every field filled.

**Ready to proceed when:** you know what decision the conversation informs, why this participant is relevant, and what answer could change your view.

## 2. Invite and open

Adapt this original invitation to truthful details:

> I'm researching how independent tutors handle lesson changes. Would you be open to a short conversation about a recent example? It is useful to hear what works well, too. I'm trying to understand the process rather than sell a product. If you're interested, what time would suit you?

Do not imply you have contacted anyone by drafting an invitation. At the start, confirm the purpose and permission to take notes. Ask separately before recording. Let the participant skip questions.

**Opening:** “Walk me through the last lesson you had to reschedule.” Follow their sequence before introducing your interpretation.

## 3. Follow evidence during the conversation

| If you hear… | Try… | Capture… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A general claim | “Can you describe a particular occasion?” | A sequence, or the fact that no example was recalled |
| A compliment about your idea | “How do you handle this today?” | Current behavior rather than approval |
| A requested feature | “What happened that made you want that?” | The underlying situation and workaround |
| A dramatic complaint | “What happened as a result?” | Consequences the participant can actually describe |
| A conflicting detail | “What was different between those occasions?” | Both accounts and their context |
| A working process | “What makes that work well?” | Evidence against the need for change |

Close by summarizing what you understood and asking for corrections. A further meeting or introduction is useful only if it answers a remaining question. Record whether it was proposed, agreed, or completed.

## 4. Turn notes into a debrief

Copy the [research debrief](../skills/customer-interview-planner/assets/research-debrief.md). Give notes IDs and link each interpretation to its evidence. Keep exact quotes distinct from paraphrases; preserve unknowns rather than estimating them for a tidier story.

Compare similar workflows separately. Record contradictions alongside supporting evidence. A teammate should be able to read the notes and disagree with your interpretation without needing your memory of the conversation.

**Ready to decide when:** you can state what changed, the strongest contrary evidence, and what is still unknown. If the notes cannot support a decision, say which missing detail matters.

## 5. Choose the next action

| What you have | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| An unclear or isolated incident | Clarify it or compare a relevant contrasting situation |
| A recurring problem with unclear consequences | Examine current workarounds and actual consequences |
| Conflicting patterns across participants | Separate the segments or situations before combining findings |
| A specific deliverable and a plausible buyer | Design a scoped offer or pilot with explicit terms |
| A process that already works adequately | Stop, narrow, or reconsider the proposed improvement |

Use the [next-test card](../skills/customer-interview-planner/assets/next-test.md) to define the action, observable signal, and what would make you stop or change direction. Do not impose a universal interview count or turn polite interest into revenue.
