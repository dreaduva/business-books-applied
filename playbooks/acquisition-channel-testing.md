# Acquisition Channel Test Plan Playbook

[Home](../README.md) · [Book guide](../books/traction-gabriel-weinberg/README.md) · [Worksheet](../books/traction-gabriel-weinberg/worksheet.md)

## 1. Name the acquisition event

Choose a customer behavior that matters and define qualification. Write the cash, time, and observation constraints before selecting channels.

## 2. Map plausible routes

List candidates with audience fit and access requirements. Include unfamiliar routes when there is a credible reason they could work. Defer channels whose timing cannot answer the current question.

## 3. Design a small shortlist

Give each proposed test an offer, destination, cap, owner, and review point. Make comparisons as fair as feasible and record unavoidable differences.

## 4. Read the entire funnel

After authorized tests, preserve raw counts, costs, hours, and missing data. Check attribution before adding customer totals. Separate acquisition from later retention.

## 5. Allocate the next round

Choose a follow-up based on fit, observed outcomes, uncertainty, and constraints. Specify what would make you stop or change the test. A channel with promising early evidence still needs confirmation that it can be reached repeatedly.

## Review the funnel before increasing the budget

Rebuild the totals from anonymous event records and mark which cohorts have completed follow-up. Compare cost per the same outcome, with hours visible. Examine repeatable access and support work before choosing the next allocation. Save the rejected interpretation as well as the choice: cheap respondents, tiny cohorts and one-off partner favors are common reasons to repeat a bounded test instead of scaling.
