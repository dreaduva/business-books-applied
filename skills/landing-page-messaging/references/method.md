# Landing-Page Messaging Brief: Method and Judgment

## A page is a promise

Check what each line commits the business to deliver. “Request a walkthrough” promises a request flow; “Start now” may imply immediate access. Explicitly identify timing, manual work, and eligibility when omission would mislead a reasonable reader.

## Evidence belongs beside the claim

A demonstration supports a capability claim. It does not prove a business outcome. Replace unsupported quantified benefits with accurate product descriptions. If a user supplies a testimonial, preserve its meaning and confirm that it is authorized for the intended use; never manufacture one to fill the layout.

## Separate three questions

Clarity asks whether the reader understands. Relevance asks whether the problem matters to them. Conversion asks whether they take the intended action under actual conditions. A comprehension exercise addresses the first and may inform the second; it cannot establish the third.

## Keep the stakes proportionate

Describe the ordinary consequences of the problem. Avoid shame, fabricated scarcity, or exaggerated threats. For a small operational product, a concrete missed handoff may explain the issue better than a dramatic transformation story. A factual, modest draft is preferable to polished copy that depends on unverified claims.
