# From Interview Notes to a Next Decision

An original analysis procedure. It does not turn a small set of interviews into a statistical market estimate.

## Preserve the evidence chain

Assign each note an ID, such as N1. Record the segment, situation, source type, reported behavior, consequence, workaround, and missing detail. Quote only exact words present in the input. A founder's interpretation is not a participant quote; a participant's account is not independent observation.

Example: “N1 reports exchanging messages after a cancellation” supports the existence of a reported coordination incident. It does not establish hours lost, weekly frequency, unmet demand, or willingness to buy.

## Compare situations before counting patterns

Separate participants by meaningful differences in workflow, purchasing role, constraints, or incident type. Do not combine a solo tutor's workflow with a school administrator's procurement problem solely because both mention scheduling.

For each tentative finding, list supporting note IDs, conflicting IDs, and unresolved questions. A single contrary incident does not erase a pattern, but it may narrow the segment or reveal a condition. If participants were recruited because they had a problem, do not extrapolate prevalence.

Repeated detail can justify another test. It cannot establish market size or a universal interview-count cutoff. If the input does not identify how many people were interviewed or whether notes refer to the same person, do not silently count them as separate participants.

## Separate signals

| Signal | Record it as | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Praise | Positive reaction | Adoption |
| “I would pay” | Stated intention | Revenue |
| Meeting proposed | Possible next step | Time already committed |
| Meeting attended | Completed action | Purchase commitment |
| Introduction completed | Access to another participant | Buyer approval |
| Letter of intent | A document whose terms need reading | Payment or a binding order |
| Payment received | Transaction under stated terms | Retention or product-market fit |

## Choose the smallest informative next move

| Finding | Useful next move | What would change the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No concrete incident | Broaden a neutral conversation or recruit for relevant recent experience | A specific, consequential incident |
| Incident, unclear frequency | Examine records or contrast ordinary and difficult periods | Evidence that the event is routine or exceptional |
| Repeated problem, adequate workaround | Understand why the workaround is retained | A meaningful unmet constraint |
| Conflicting segments | Separate the workflows and compare their constraints | A coherent segment-specific pattern |
| Important problem, unknown buyer | Investigate the purchasing process | A named role and actual decision path |
| Specific deliverable can be offered | Propose a clearly scoped pilot with agreed terms | Actual acceptance, use, and results |

Use the next-test card to name the assumption, action, observable evidence, decision rule, stop condition, and owner. Leave unchosen commercial terms as decisions to make; do not invent a price and present it as customer evidence. No single next step is mandatory for every interview.

## Share a debrief

State the decision, what changed, the strongest contrary evidence, and the next action. Attach traceable notes so another reader can challenge the interpretation. Recommend revisiting assumptions when new information arrives; do not quietly rewrite old evidence to fit the latest product idea.
