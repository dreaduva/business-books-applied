# The Mom Test Worked Example: Tutor Scheduling

[Blank interview plan](../assets/interview-plan.md) · [Evidence and decisions](evidence-and-decisions.md)

**Fictional teaching example.** Every participant, conversation, note, and result below is invented to demonstrate the workflow. This is original project material, not a case from the book. The completed plan follows the fields in the blank template.

## Learning objective

- **Decision this research should inform:** whether to investigate lesson-change coordination before building a replacement calendar.
- **Target customer and relevant situation:** independent tutors arranging individual lessons directly with students or parents.
- **What we believe but have not established:** schedule changes cause recurring, poorly served coordination work; a new calendar would address it.
- **Evidence that would change our mind:** changes are uncommon, existing tools handle them adequately, or the difficulty comes from communication among people rather than the calendar.

## Participant selection

- **Relevant recent experience:** a tutor who can recall rearranging an individual lesson. Include both parent-mediated and adult-student workflows.
- **How we will recruit:** ask a tutoring community moderator whether a voluntary research invitation is appropriate, then invite relevant tutors without claiming the problem is widespread. This is a proposed route, not outreach already performed.
- **Perspectives this sample may miss:** agency coordinators, tutors outside that community, people without recent changes, and people who decline research invitations. Recruiting for recent changes cannot establish their frequency across all tutors.
- **Privacy and recording arrangements, if relevant:** ask permission to take notes; do not record by default. Use participant aliases and avoid copying student or parent identifiers.

## Conversation guide

- **Opening context, without a product pitch:** “I'm trying to understand how tutors handle lesson changes. Your current setup may work well; that is useful to hear too. Is it okay if I take notes? You can skip anything.”
- **Prompt about a recent incident:** “Walk me through the last lesson you had to reschedule.”
- **Follow-up about the sequence of actions:** “What happened after you received the request?”
- **Follow-up about tools and workarounds:** “Where did you update the time, and how did the other people find out?”
- **Follow-up about time, money, or another concrete consequence:** “What, if anything, did that change affect?” If extra work is mentioned: “Can you reconstruct what you had to do?”
- **Follow-up about alternatives tried:** “What have you done differently on other occasions? What made you keep or change that approach?”
- **Closing question and appropriate next step:** “What have I misunderstood about that example? If I need to clarify how confirmations work, would you be open to another short conversation?” Record the response; do not assume agreement.

## Review after the conversation

- **What surprised us:** editing the calendar was straightforward; waiting for another person's confirmation was the uncertain step.
- **What contradicted our assumption:** A described the incident as unusual, and B described an adequate process.
- **Which questions were leading or unclear:** the initial draft “How much time do you waste on messages?” assumed both waste and a measurable cost. We replaced it with “What, if anything, did that change affect?”
- **What needs a follow-up:** reconstruct ordinary and exceptional confirmation sequences for parent-mediated lessons, including whether anyone actually missed a lesson.
- **What this evidence cannot establish:** prevalence, willingness to pay, market size, or whether a new product would improve the process.
- **Next investigation and reason:** examine redacted confirmation sequences with willing tutors in parent-mediated workflows, including an ordinary successful change. This tests whether the apparent coordination gap recurs without presuming a new calendar is needed.

## Illustrative note

The critical contrast is **N1 versus N2**: an awkward event happened, but the same participant says it was unusual. Keep both in the record. N3 supplies a contrasting workflow, not a vote that cancels A's experience.

## Completed research debrief

**Decision:** do not build a replacement calendar on this evidence. Investigate the narrower confirmation workflow.

**Input:** three notes from two invented participants, selected for recent rescheduling experience. None is an independent observation or purchase signal.

| Tentative finding | Supporting IDs | Conflicting IDs | Interpretation | Still unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmation among several people can become unclear | N1 | N2 limits frequency; N3 has a different, working arrangement | Investigate the conditions under which coordination fails. | Consequences and recurrence. |
| A replacement calendar is necessary | None | N1 reports successful editing; N3 reports a working calendar invitation | The current feature proposal is unsupported. | Whether another unmet need exists. |
| Tutors will pay for a solution | None | No relevant purchase evidence supplied | No conclusion. | Buyer, alternatives, budget, and an actual offer. |

**Strongest contrary evidence:** the only difficult incident was described as exceptional.

**Evidence that would reverse the decision:** comparable real incidents showing recurring consequences despite adequate use of existing tools would justify considering a narrowly scoped solution test. Evidence that existing workflows reliably handle the situation would support stopping this line of inquiry.

**Owner and review point:** the fictional founder, after reviewing the next set of consented workflow records; no completion date or future result is invented.

## Completed next-test card

- **Decision this test informs:** continue investigating parent-mediated confirmation, or stop.
- **Assumption being tested:** some confirmation failures persist beyond isolated unusual circumstances.
- **Evidence already available, with note IDs:** N1 suggests a gap; N2 challenges recurrence; N3 shows a working alternative in another arrangement.
- **Participant or situation:** willing tutors who coordinate with a student and a parent; include uneventful changes as well as difficult ones.
- **Test action and actual deliverable, if any:** reconstruct a redacted sequence of messages and calendar changes with the tutor. Produce a timeline of who knew the final time and when; no software or payment is offered.
- **Observable signal:** whether an actual sequence contains an unresolved confirmation gap and what consequence followed.
- **What would support proceeding:** a recurring condition across independently described comparable situations with consequences the current process does not address; assess alternative explanations before deciding.
- **What would support stopping or changing direction:** ordinary changes are handled adequately, or the apparent gap is explained by isolated circumstances.
- **Alternative explanations to check:** travel, delayed replies that do not matter, incorrect calendar usage, and mismatched expectations about confirmation.
- **Unresolved terms or permissions:** participants must agree to any review of records; redact other people's details and avoid retaining unnecessary copies.
- **Owner and review point:** the fictional founder, when the workflow review is complete.
