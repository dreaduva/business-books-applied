# Obviously Awesome Worked Example: From Management Platform to a Specific Tracking Tool

**Fictional teaching example.** LessonLoop, Maya, all product facts, participant notes, feedback, and decisions below are invented. They illustrate the method and are not customer evidence, a case from the book, or results from the other fictional exercises in this repository. This is a standalone product scenario; no new feature or validation is implied by another book's example.

## 1. Scope and raw inputs

Maya wants to decide how to describe the current LessonLoop prototype before drafting its homepage. Her initial description is:

> LessonLoop is an AI-powered tutoring management platform that eliminates missed lessons and gives every education business complete control.

That sentence is an invented draft, not a customer quote. It contains several claims that need checking. The decision concerns today's product, not the company's eventual ambition.

### Product facts supplied to the exercise

| ID | Supplied fact | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | The prototype stores a changed lesson time and a manually entered confirmation status | Recording the status of a lesson change |
| P2 | It filters unresolved changes into one list | A consolidated pending view |
| P3 | A tutor manually enters changes and updates replies | A material user-effort requirement |
| P4 | It does not send messages, read replies, book lessons, bill customers, or store student records | Boundaries that category and messaging must respect |
| P5 | There is no AI feature and no measured attendance outcome | Neither the AI claim nor the missed-lesson claim is supported |

The prototype facts can establish what the tool does in this fictional exercise. They cannot establish customer demand.

### Buyer-context notes supplied to the exercise

| ID | Source type | Note | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N1 | Fictional interview report | Tutor A uses a calendar and messages. During weeks with several changes, checking which replies remain outstanding requires opening multiple threads. | A report from one situation; frequency and time cost are unmeasured |
| N2 | Fictional interview report | Tutor B uses an existing booking platform with a confirmation view and reports that it is adequate. | No independent audit of the platform or workflow |
| N3 | Fictional interview report | Tutor C rarely changes lesson times and keeps track from memory. Another status list sounds like more work. | Relevant contrary evidence, not a verdict on every tutor |
| N4 | Fictional sales-conversation note | Center D asks about invoices, permissions, and student records after hearing “management platform.” | Demonstrates one interpretation of that description |
| N5 | Founder research note | Maya found several scheduling apps online, but none is identified in the supplied buyer notes as a shortlisted replacement. | A watchlist, not evidence of actual consideration |

The notes deliberately include unsuitable situations. Deleting N2 and N3 would make the opportunity seem broader than the supplied evidence warrants.

## 2. Reconstruct the alternatives

For Tutor A's situation, the relevant alternative is the existing calendar-and-message routine. It already handles booking and communication. LessonLoop would supplement that routine with a recorded-status view; it would not replace the calendar or messaging service.

For Tutor B, the alternative already supplies a confirmation view. The current record does not show a compelling reason to switch. “Our interface is better” would be an unsupported rescue claim.

For Tutor C, continuing the present routine is plausible because changes are infrequent. The new tool might impose more maintenance than the problem warrants. “Doing nothing” here means retaining a working routine, not failing to recognize the founder's insight.

N5 stays on the watchlist. Maya can investigate those apps later if relevant buyers consider them, but she cannot position against invented weaknesses in products she has not evaluated.

**Decision:** develop an initial brief for the situation in N1. Retain N2 and N3 as boundary conditions and N4 as evidence that the broad category creates the wrong expectations. This is a provisional focus, not a validated market segment.

## 3. Build the capability-to-value chain

| Capability | Relevant comparison | Direct consequence | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pending filter, P2 | Opening separate threads to find recorded status, N1 | Review unresolved items together | Demonstrable view; no measured time saving |
| Manual status record, P1/P3 | Relying on thread context or memory | Keep an explicit status alongside a changed time | Only useful if the tutor maintains it |
| Narrow tracking scope, P4 | Replacing a wider booking system | Potentially use alongside current tools | Integration and convenience are not established |

Maya rejects “eliminates missed lessons.” A visible pending record might help someone notice an unresolved change, but attendance also depends on actions outside this tool. There are no attendance measurements. She also rejects “automatic confirmations”: P3 and P4 directly contradict it.

The remaining value thesis is modest but specific: a tutor handling several changing lesson times may find it useful to review recorded unresolved items in one place. The main counterargument is manual upkeep. A good brief must preserve that disadvantage rather than bury it under a claim of effortless coordination.

## 4. Choose the initial fit boundary

The candidate best-fit situation combines several observable conditions:

- The person coordinates changed lesson times through separate messages.
- They sometimes need to review several unresolved changes together.
- Their existing tools do not already supply an adequate view.
- They are willing to record status manually during an exploratory trial.

Those are proposed screening criteria, not established facts about every person in the segment. Tutor A has supplied part of the rationale; willingness to maintain a second record remains unknown.

The poor-fit cases are specific: B already has a satisfactory view, C has too few changes to establish a meaningful burden, and D needs operational capabilities the product lacks. A future center-focused product might be sensible, but it would involve a different scope and buying process. This brief cannot make that strategy true merely by including centers in the audience sentence.

## 5. Compare the descriptions

| Candidate | Reason to consider | Problem in the supplied record | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tutoring management platform | Familiar broad business context | N4 expects invoices and records absent under P4 | Reject for the current product |
| Scheduling software | Connects to changed lesson times | Suggests booking or availability features absent under P4 | Reject as the lead description |
| Lesson-confirmation tracker | Describes the narrow recorded-status task | May still imply automatic collection of replies | Select provisionally, with manual behavior explicit |

“Lesson-confirmation tracker” is a descriptive phrase in this exercise. No evidence is supplied that it is an established market category. Maya therefore does not call LessonLoop the category leader or claim there is a recognized market of a particular size.

The choice is based on the product and buyer situation, not a numerical scoring formula. The remaining ambiguity becomes a question to test.

## 6. First brief and claim audit

**Brief version A:** For independent tutors coordinating changed lesson times through messages, LessonLoop is a lesson-confirmation tracker that shows unresolved confirmations together. It provides a pending view alongside the tutor's existing calendar and messages.

This is closer to the facts, but it still omits how the list gets populated. Maya records the following audit before translating it into homepage copy:

| Claim | Action | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI-powered | Remove | P5 says there is no AI feature |
| Management platform | Replace | Broad expected capabilities are absent |
| Eliminates missed lessons | Remove | No evidence of attendance outcomes |
| For every education business | Narrow | N2–N4 challenge breadth and current scope |
| Shows unresolved confirmations together | Retain with qualification | P2 supports the view; P3 requires manual upkeep |
| Works alongside existing tools | Explain concretely | No integration is implied or demonstrated |

The brief remains provisional because the value of the extra recording work has not been established.

## 7. Feedback reveals a second problem

Maya presents version A to fictional relevant reader R1 and asks, “What would you expect this product to do, and what would you have to do yourself?” R1 expects replies to appear automatically after parents answer messages. Maya records that answer before explaining the actual product.

A second fictional reader, R2, understands that it is a status list but asks why they would maintain another record. These reactions point to different issues:

- R1 exposes a **comprehension mismatch**: version A suggests automation the product does not provide.
- R2 exposes a **value objection**: the described benefit may not outweigh the work even when understood.

Maya must not treat both as a copywriting problem. Clarifying manual entry can address R1's misunderstanding. It cannot establish that R2 should find the product worthwhile.

## 8. Revised brief and next decision

**Brief version B:** For independent tutors who coordinate changed lesson times through separate messages and need a view of outstanding replies, LessonLoop is a manual lesson-confirmation tracker. Tutors record each change and update its status when a reply arrives, then review unresolved items together. It supplements the existing calendar and messaging routine; it does not send messages or collect replies automatically.

**Changed:** manual recording is explicit; the audience is tied to the need for an outstanding-reply view; automation expectations are corrected.

**Unchanged:** the current alternative remains separate messages, the capability remains a pending view, and there is still no quantified time-saving or attendance claim. The product itself has not changed.

Maya chooses to use version B as the next provisional description. She does not call it validated or claim that the two fictional responses establish a market pattern.

Next evidence: ask relevant readers to explain version B without coaching, then observe whether a suitable tutor can use the status-recording workflow and finds the extra record worthwhile. Keep comprehension and workflow usefulness separate. Any commercial test would require a defined offer and actual buying evidence.

If people understand the manual work but consistently prefer their existing routine, the next decision may concern the product's value or target situation rather than the wording. If a real shortlisted alternative already provides the same view with less effort, revisit the differentiation. If the product later genuinely automates reply capture, revise P3/P4 and the affected claim chain before changing the public promise.

## What this example teaches

The final brief is narrower than the opening pitch because the facts support a narrower product. Its usefulness comes from explaining the choice and preserving the objections. No sentence can establish a benefit the product and customer evidence do not support.

The traceable chain is P1–P5 and N1–N5 → alternative selection → capability/value comparison → fit boundary → description choice → feedback → version B. A reviewer can challenge any link without having to accept Maya's conclusion as a whole.
