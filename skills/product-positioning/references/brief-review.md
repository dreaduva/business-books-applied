# Reviewing and Revising a Positioning Brief

Use for an existing brief, a category disagreement, or supplied reader/customer feedback. Preserve the original version and explain revisions rather than silently replacing the argument.

## Audit claims by their consequence

For each material claim, identify the evidence and the unsupported inference, if any. Return a concrete repair.

| Claim pattern | Check | Useful repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “AI-powered” | Does a working AI behavior exist and matter here? | Remove or describe the actual capability |
| “Saves 50%” | What was measured, for whom, and against what? | Use the supported comparison or retain the figure as unsubstantiated |
| “For everyone” | Do alternatives and value actually match across groups? | Separate situations and choose a defensible focus |
| “Only solution” | Is the scope of comparison complete enough? | Limit the claim to demonstrated differences from named alternatives |
| Broad management category | Can the product meet its central expectations? | Compare a narrower category or descriptive phrase |

Prioritize mismatches that change the buying expectation, not minor stylistic preferences. A critique should produce an improved brief with residual uncertainty, not merely a list of forbidden phrases.

## Separate feedback types

A person expecting automatic replies from a manual tracker signals a comprehension problem. A person who understands the manual tracker and prefers their existing routine signals a relevance or switching-burden problem. A person who wants it but cannot approve a purchase signals a buying-process constraint. Different remedies follow.

Record the version shown, participant situation, prompt, and actual response. If feedback is paraphrased, do not invent quotation marks. Do not merge responses to different versions into a single success score. Preserve contradictions between participants and compare their circumstances before averaging them away.

## Test the reasoning backward

For each proposed value, identify its supporting capability and relevant alternative. If the alternative already provides the capability, ask what additional difference is supported. If none is supplied, mark the differentiation unresolved instead of inventing a superiority story.

For each fit criterion, ask why it increases the importance of the consequence. A demographic label may be insufficient. For each category, name the expectations it creates and whether they are accurate. The brief should survive these checks without hidden roadmap features.

## Revise and choose the next evidence

State changed and unchanged components. A new product fact can justify a capability revision; a misunderstanding can justify clarifying the description; a consistent value objection may justify examining the product or segment. Do not treat all objections as copy problems.

Present the revised brief, the remaining claim limits, and a specific observation that could overturn the recommendation. Comprehension checks ask what the reader thinks the product does and what work they expect. Relevance checks examine the existing routine and whether the consequence matters. Commercial tests require a defined offer and actual buying behavior. Do not equate these evidence types.

Keep the next action proportionate. Draft questions or a review plan without contacting anyone or publishing a revision unless separately authorized.
