# Traction Worked Example: Cheap Leads, Weak Follow-Through

**Fictional teaching example.** Every organization, event and number below is invented. These are original application materials, not outcomes from the book or a real business.

## The decision and the constraints

LessonLoop is a manual lesson-confirmation tracker for independent tutors. Its founder wants to choose the next acquisition test, not estimate a lifetime acquisition cost. The target is tutors who handled at least three lesson changes last month. The meaningful near-term event is adding a real changed lesson and returning to check it. There is no paid offer yet, so no result below is customer acquisition cost.

The founder has a $200 cash ceiling and twelve hours for initial tests and follow-up. A newsletter can accept one placement; a tutor association can host one demonstration; a new search article can be published but has no established ranking. The offer is a prototype walkthrough. No route promises automation.

## Broad consideration and shortlist

A search ad was deferred because the founder had no verified useful query or landing-page conversion baseline. A trade show exceeded the current budget. Referral incentives were premature with no active customer base. Those deferrals are resource decisions, not proof those channels cannot work.

The shortlist contains a specialist newsletter, an association demonstration and a useful search article. Each reaches the intended work context differently. The article is a long-horizon exploration; the founder agrees in advance not to judge its full potential after two weeks.

The provisional repeat rule for the two immediate routes is at least two qualified people who activate, with a feasible path to repeat access. This is a small learning threshold, not a validated benchmark. The founder will also inspect repeat use and cost before allocating the next week.

## Test cards

| Route | Test | Cap | Observation |

| Newsletter | One approved placement linking to the walkthrough page | $120; 3 hours | Responses for 7 days; each activation observed for a further 7 days |
| Association | One agreed demonstration for tutors | $40; 6 hours | Same activation and follow-up definitions |
| Search article | Publish one useful lesson-change checklist with a relevant product link | $0; 3 hours | Two-week early check; acquisition potential remains open |

The twelve-hour limit includes setup, delivery and follow-up. Publisher reach estimates are not treated as verified unique exposure. Landing visits are counted separately from respondents. Attribution uses the person's reported first discovery route; uncertain or overlapping cases remain flagged.

## Raw fictional records

Twenty newsletter respondents were recorded. N01–N05 met the qualification rule; N06–N20 did not meet it or explicitly wanted only the free checklist. N01 and N02 activated. At their seven-day follow-up, N01 had returned; N02 reported reverting to a paper list.

The association produced five respondents, P01–P05. P01–P04 qualified. P01–P03 activated and all three returned at follow-up. P04 did not activate; P05 did not meet the lesson-change criterion. P02 also read the newsletter after the event and is retained under association first discovery, not counted twice.

The search article had twelve tracked visits and one respondent, S01, who did not qualify. Search indexing and future distribution are not established by this result.

## Calculation and interpretation

Newsletter cash per respondent is $120 / 20 = $6. Association is $40 / 5 = $8. Choosing by that number would favor the newsletter. But cash per qualified respondent is $24 versus $10; cash per activation is $60 versus about $13.33. The downstream difference changes the question.

At an explicitly assumed $30 per founder hour, total test cost is $210 for newsletter, $220 for association, and $90 for the article. Loaded cost per activation is $105 versus about $73.33. The article has zero activations, so it has no finite observed cost per activation; calling it “free acquisition” would hide both the hours and the absent result.

Newsletter qualification is 5/20 respondents; association qualification is 4/5. Returning use is 1/2 versus 3/3 activated users. The raw counts matter more than a polished percentage: these cohorts are tiny, and the association included a live explanation the newsletter did not. Better results could reflect that extra support, a better audience, or both.

## Initial proposal, challenge and revision

The founder's first proposal was to buy four more newsletter placements because its leads were cheapest. The qualification and usage records reject that justification. Most respondents were poor fits, and buying four placements would exceed the original test budget without resolving the mismatch.

The second proposal was to declare partnerships the winning channel. That is also too strong. One association host has offered access once. It may not repeat, and six hours per event could become the bottleneck.

The revised decision is a single association repeat, contingent on another suitable host agreeing. The founder will spend no more than $60 and five hours, reuse the same offer and qualification rule, and record setup versus delivery versus follow-up time. The original article stays available but receives no new production time until the scheduled longer-horizon review.

## Completed next-test brief

**Question:** Can a second association encounter bring at least two qualified activations without exceeding five founder hours?

**Audience and offer:** Independent tutors with at least three recent lesson changes; a demonstration of the manual pending list.

**Access prerequisite:** A host confirms the audience and date. Without this, status is “access not obtained,” not “customers rejected the offer.”

**Measurement:** Anonymous respondent ID, first discovery, qualification, activation date, seven-day return, and founder time by activity. Keep the newsletter overlap flag.

**Decision:** If access repeats, two qualified activations occur and follow-up fits the cap, consider another bounded repeat. If activations require extensive personal setup, test that bottleneck before increasing event frequency. If appropriate attendees understand the offer but reject manual tracking, return to product/positioning research.

**Unknowns:** Paid demand, channel scale, stable conversion rates, long-term retention and search potential remain unknown. The next test has not run.
