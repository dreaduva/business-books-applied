# Habit Experiment Plan: Method and Judgment

## Make the behavior observable

Choose an action small enough to start under ordinary conditions but meaningful enough to support the goal. “Open the spreadsheet” may be an entry step; it is not evidence that the business review occurred. State the smallest useful result.

## Match the cue to reality

An unreliable event makes a poor anchor. If a user works shifts, avoid assuming a fixed morning routine. A context-based cue can work better, but it also needs a fallback for the moments when that context changes.

## Track opportunities and quality

Keep the denominator visible. A habit performed after meetings should be assessed against relevant meetings, not days with no meeting. Include a lightweight quality check so repeated activity does not replace useful work.

## Treat misses as information

Distinguish forgetting, missing materials, conflicting obligations, and an action that was too large. These suggest different adjustments. Avoid shame and escalating punishment. A short experiment can reveal fit and friction; it does not establish automaticity or promise outcomes outside the behavior itself.
