# Atomic Habits Worked Example

**Fictional teaching example.** All organizations, participants, numbers, and outcomes are invented for this exercise, not reported results or a case from the book.

## Behavior and purpose

Fictional founder Maya wants interview evidence to remain usable. Behavior: after each customer call, capture one observed problem with its source. Minimum: one factual note, distinguishing what the person did from Maya's interpretation. Opportunities occur when interviews happen, not every calendar day.

## Cue and environment

Cue: the interview call ends. Location: the same desk. Before the call, open the interview-note template beside the call window. Close the inbox until the minimum note is captured. If the call ends away from the desk, capture a short note in the designated notes app rather than waiting for a perfect setup.

## Small action and feedback

Write one observed event and the participant reference, then mark “captured.” Optional extension: add uncertainties and follow-up questions. The minimum must not turn an inference into a customer statement. A checked box counts only when a usable note exists, not merely when the document is opened.

## Recovery and observation

If interrupted, capture the note at the next available work break without pretending the details are certain. Mark uncertain recollections. Resume the normal cue at the next call. Observe the next six interview opportunities. Log opportunity, completion, obstacle, and whether the note is useful during review. No claim that six repetitions form a habit.

## Review and next adjustment

Invented trial: five of six opportunities produced notes; one was missed after an immediate support call. Four notes contained a concrete event, while one contained only an opinion. Revise the template prompt to ask “What happened last time?” and reserve a short gap after interviews where feasible. Retain the event cue. This is a fictional adjustment, not evidence of general effectiveness.
