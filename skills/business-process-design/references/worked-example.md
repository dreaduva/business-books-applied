# The E-Myth Revisited Worked Example

**Fictional teaching example.** All organizations, participants, numbers, and outcomes are invented for this exercise, not reported results or a case from the book.

## Process boundary

Process: onboard a fictional LessonLoop pilot. Trigger: the customer has accepted the agreed pilot terms and requested setup. End: the customer can view a sample pending confirmation and knows how to update it. Excludes payment disputes, importing student records, and custom integrations.

## Owner and prerequisites

Maya owns the process; a trained support operator may execute it. Inputs: confirmed customer contact, agreed pilot scope, and access to a test workspace. Use sample data during the demonstration. If acceptance of terms or workspace access is missing, pause and ask the owner; do not invent consent or share credentials.

## Operating steps

1. Check the pilot scope and setup request.
2. Create or open the authorized customer workspace using the normal access procedure.
3. Add a clearly marked sample lesson change.
4. Demonstrate pending and confirmed statuses.
5. Ask the customer to update the sample status themselves.
6. Explain manual updates, support limits, and where to ask for help.
7. Remove sample data when appropriate and record completion or the blocking issue.

## Acceptance and exceptions

Acceptance: the customer successfully changes the sample status and can explain which updates remain manual. If access fails, leave onboarding incomplete and route it to Maya. If the customer expects automated messaging, clarify scope before proceeding. Requests to upload sensitive student records are outside this pilot; stop and refer to the owner rather than improvising a data process.

## Handoff test and revision

Fictional desk walkthrough: the operator reaches “workspace access” and cannot determine who grants it. Revision: prerequisite now requires an authorized test workspace and names Maya as escalation owner. Version 1.0, owner Maya. A real handoff trial remains proposed. Review after the first authorized onboarding or when product access changes; record pauses and failures, not just duration.
