# Offer delivery decisions

Itemize preparation, live work, handoff, administration and support. Sum the hours before dividing capacity. Deduct the user's stated buffer; do not invent unlimited availability. Preserve direct cash and an optional stated labor valuation separately.

Show price minus direct cash, then subtract valued labor if available. Name excluded overhead/acquisition/tax costs; do not call the remainder net profit. If hours or prices are missing, give the formula and the specific missing inputs, not fabricated margins.

Compare one plausible overrun with the base case. A negative contribution or capacity breach requires an explicit choice about scope, delivery, staffing or a price hypothesis. Do not imply a higher price will be accepted.

Audit promises by control. A configured artifact is different from a business outcome depending on customers or third parties. Replace unsupported guarantees with a precise scope and acceptance point; do not draft jurisdiction-specific legal terms. Capacity-based urgency needs an actual inventory and timing fact.

For objections, identify whether the issue is understanding, effort, trust, relevance or ongoing product limitations. Return changed terms/copy and an evidence plan tied to that diagnosis. Planning an offer does not authorize publishing it, contacting buyers or taking payments.
