# $100M Offers Worked Example: A Deliverable Pilot

**Fictional teaching example.** All prices, people, capacities and observations are invented. This is an original offer-design exercise, not a reported business result or financial forecast.

## Input facts and unresolved demand

A fictional solo operator helps tutors set up LessonLoop, a manual confirmation tracker. The current product supports adding lesson changes and updating their status. It does not read messages, automate reminders or prevent no-shows. The operator can allocate twelve hours per week to delivery, including a two-hour buffer.

A demonstration exists; paying-customer evidence does not. The proposed buyer is a tutor with several changed lessons per week who wants help creating an initial list. Whether that buyer will pay remains untested. All price figures below are hypotheses in dollars, not recommendations for a real market.

## From obstacle to deliverable

| Hypothesized obstacle | Proposed help | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does not know how to structure the first list | Prepare a list from up to ten customer-supplied changes | Customer supplies accurate details |
| Unsure how to update a reply | Demonstrate one update during the call | No ongoing message monitoring |
| Forgets the steps after the call | One-page operating note | No large unrelated bonus library |
| Has a question after setup | One follow-up reply within the defined support window | Additional custom work is outside scope |

The acceptance point is a list the customer can update and explain. It is not reduced no-shows or a measured time saving. That keeps the intended benefit separate from the result the operator actually controls.

## Three versions, with arithmetic

| Version | Proposed price | Direct cash cost | Delivery hours | Estimate after cash and labor at $30/hour |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A: self-service guide | $29 | $4 | 0.5 | $10 |
| B: assisted setup | $99 | $9 | 2 | $30 |
| C: managed week | $149 | $12 | 6 | -$43 |

A's estimate is $29 - $4 - (0.5 × $30) = $10. B is $99 - $9 - (2 × $30) = $30. C is $149 - $12 - (6 × $30) = -$43. These exclude acquisition, fixed overhead, taxes and other costs. They are scenario contributions, not profit.

C sounds generous because it transfers work from the customer, but it also assumes six delivery hours and exceeds the price under this labor assumption. A is cheaper but leaves the setup obstacle mostly with the buyer. B is selected for a small test because it directly addresses setup and has a bounded delivery path. This is a reasoned choice under assumptions, not an observed preference.

## What the two hours include

- 20 minutes: review supplied inputs and identify missing fields.
- 45 minutes: setup call and first update.
- 15 minutes: handoff note.
- 20 minutes: follow-up allowance.
- 20 minutes: scheduling and administration.

Total: 120 minutes. With ten hours available after the weekly buffer, the arithmetic ceiling is five B customers. That is not a launch commitment. The operator proposes only two initial slots to learn actual delivery time while retaining room for overruns and other responsibilities.

## The first offer and why it fails review

Version 1: “For $99, we'll organize your lesson changes and guarantee no missed lessons. Unlimited help included. Only two spots left!”

The setup claim is too vague; the attendance guarantee depends on student behavior; unlimited support invalidates the two-hour estimate. “Two spots left” also implies an inventory claim that requires current booking information. The operator has decided on two initial slots, but there is no basis to imply others have already been sold.

A simulated objection makes the failure concrete: “Will you check my messages every day and chase anyone who doesn't reply?” The answer is no. Adding a reminder template does not fix a promise that implies a managed service.

## Revised buyer-facing offer

### Set up your first lesson-change tracking list

A $99 assisted setup for an independent tutor who wants a manual list of outstanding replies.

Bring up to ten current lesson changes. In a 45-minute call, we will help you enter them, show you how to update a status when a reply arrives, and leave you with a short operating note. The package includes one follow-up question by email within seven days of the call.

You continue sending messages and updating the list yourself. This package does not monitor replies, send reminders or guarantee attendance. The setup call is scheduled after we confirm that the required lesson details are available.

We are testing two initial setup slots this week. Request a slot to confirm fit and availability before payment. If the proposed scope does not fit your needs, we will say so before booking.

**Action:** Request an assisted setup slot.

Payment handling, cancellation terms and any delivery remedy must be settled before offering this publicly. This is a draft teaching offer, not a live checkout or finished legal agreement.

## Stress test and operating revision

Suppose each customer needs one extra support hour. B's labor-adjusted contribution becomes $99 - $9 - (3 × $30) = $0 before overhead and acquisition. With ten delivery hours, only three such customers fit, leaving one hour. The earlier ceiling of five no longer holds.

The revision is not simply “work faster.” Before booking, confirm the customer accepts manual maintenance and can supply the inputs. Keep the one follow-up question explicit. Track actual support requests and do not silently refuse work that was promised. If the bounded package still takes three hours, reconsider delivery design or proposed price before the next cohort.

A possible $129 price would leave $30 under the same three-hour scenario, but no buyer has accepted that price. It belongs in the next decision discussion, not in a claim that the offer is now validated.

## Finished test brief

**Question:** Will suitable tutors choose this bounded setup offer, and can the operator deliver it in two hours per customer?

**Proposed test:** Offer two clearly scoped slots through an authorized channel after commercial terms and the payment flow are ready. Record acceptance/rejection reasons and delivery minutes by activity.

**Delivery check:** Customer can add and update a change at handoff; note any missing input or unplanned work. Inspect follow-up after the seven-day window.

**Decision:** If buyers expect ongoing management, revise fit and scope before another sale. If the task is valued but overruns, diagnose the specific activity before increasing capacity. If buyers understand and prefer their existing list, investigate value rather than adding unrelated bonuses.

No outreach, sales or delivery happened in this example. Demand and actual costs remain open.
