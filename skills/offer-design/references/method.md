# Offer Design Brief: Method and Judgment

## Improve the work, then the promise

Reducing customer effort may shift work to the provider. Record who does the work and how much capacity it consumes. A more attractive offer that cannot be fulfilled is not an improvement.

## Keep value qualitative

Outcome desirability and perceived credibility are not objective quantities supplied by a formula. Use the framework to surface uncertainties. A user asking for a numerical value score should receive an explanation of why the supplied inputs do not justify one.

## State the accounting boundary

Show price, direct costs, assumed labor valuation, and excluded expenses separately. With unknown costs, return a range or an incomplete estimate rather than a margin claim. Do not imply that a price validated by arithmetic is validated by buyers.

## Make commitments concrete

Limits should reflect actual capacity or scope. A guarantee needs a defined trigger and fulfillment ability; missing terms remain open. Do not create fake deadlines or imply demand that has not occurred. The next demand test should record actual behavior while separately checking whether delivery stays within the expected workload.
