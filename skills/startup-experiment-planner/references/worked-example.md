# The Lean Startup Worked Example

**Fictional teaching example.** All organizations, participants, numbers, and outcomes are invented for this exercise, not reported results or a case from the book.

## Decision and assumption

Decision: whether to automate parent confirmations. Assumption: a manual confirmation service reduces unresolved changes without excessive coordination. Existing evidence: the prior fictional tutor interviews suggest occasional ambiguity, not established demand.

## Intervention and participants

Four fictional tutors with parent-mediated lessons opt into a two-week manual pilot. The founder explicitly handles confirmations; no automation is promised. Participants agree to minimal data handling and can withdraw.

## Measurement

Unit: eligible lesson changes during the pilot. Measure whether the relevant parties acknowledged the final time. Track founder minutes per event. Compare with tutors' preceding records, noting different events and no random assignment.

## Decision rules

The fictional founder chooses to investigate automation only if no event remains unresolved and median manual effort is at most five minutes. Any unresolved event requires reviewing its cause. These are illustrative operating constraints, not statistical significance or universal thresholds. Review after two weeks.

## Result and interpretation

Invented outcome: 10 of 12 eligible changes were acknowledged; two remained unresolved. Median effort was eight minutes per event. Both predeclared constraints failed. Review the two failures and narrow the workflow before automating. The nonrandom comparison cannot attribute all change to the service, and willingness to pay remains unknown.
